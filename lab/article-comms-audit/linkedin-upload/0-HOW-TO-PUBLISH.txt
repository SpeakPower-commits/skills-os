PUBLISHING ON LINKEDIN: text and images are separate on purpose
=================================================================

Pasting from Word, a PDF or a web page carries hidden formatting and embedded
images, which is the likely reason your paste kept failing. Here the words are
plain text, and every image is its own small file you add by hand.

Files
  1-article-text.txt      the article, plain text, no images inside
  2-feed-post.txt         the short teaser post for your feed
  images\                 1-cover.png, 2-audience-chart.png, 3-six-checks.png,
                          4-thirty-minute-checklist.png
                          (extra-cover-photo.jpg and extra-author-photo.png are optional)

Steps for the article
  1. Open a new LinkedIn article (Write article).
  2. Upload images\1-cover.png as the cover image.
  3. Open 1-article-text.txt in Notepad, press Ctrl+A, Ctrl+C.
  4. Click into the article body and paste with Ctrl+Shift+V (paste as plain text).
     Paste the title line into the title box. If the paste still fails, paste one
     section at a time.
  5. Delete the [COVER IMAGE ...] line.
  6. Find each [INSERT IMAGE ...] line. Delete it, then add that image with the
     editor's image button, in this order:
       2-audience-chart.png          after "And founders are exactly who I say I'm speaking to."
       3-six-checks.png              after the six numbered checks
       4-thirty-minute-checklist.png after "The one number (5 minutes)."
  7. Select each of these lines and give it the Heading style:
       The message was fine. The match was wrong.
       But isn't a wider audience better?
       What a communications audit actually checks
       Why this hits NGOs and young companies harder
       Run a mini-audit in 30 minutes
       What I'm doing about it
  8. Optional: bold the six numbered check names and the four step names.
  9. Preview, then publish when you are happy.

Then the feed post
  1. Start a new post, paste 2-feed-post.txt as plain text.
  2. Replace [article link] with the link to the published article.
  3. Attach images\2-audience-chart.png.

Before you publish, check the numbers (715 followers, 29% entry-level, 6% founders)
against your LinkedIn analytics. See ..\README.md.
