## Check these before it goes live

1. **The research claims.** They rest on what I read in the papers' own text, not on the full papers.
   - Original: Small, Loewenstein and Slovic (2007), Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes 102(2). People could donate real money they had earned; the one named child raised more; adding statistics beside the child reduced giving.
   - Repeat: Maier, Wong and Feldman (2023), Collabra: Psychology 9(1), https://doi.org/10.1525/collabra.90203. Pre-registered repeat of Studies 1 and 3 of the original. No support for the effect. Donations were hypothetical, which the authors flag. They suggest scope insensitivity as a better frame.
   - The article says "two of the four original experiments". The repeat says the original had four and it focused on Studies 1 and 3.
   - I did not read the 2016 meta-analysis (Lee and Feeley, Social Influence) beyond its title, so it is not cited. Someone may point to it. Read its abstract before you reply.
2. **"I've nodded along to it for years."** An assumption about your past advice. Change it if untrue.
3. **"My own storytelling arc starts with a hero."** Taken from the five-part arc in `speakpower-comms-strategist`.
4. **"I haven't found a study that tests funders reading proposals."** True of my search, which was short. Do not claim more.
5. **The scope-insensitivity line** ("your 10,000 beneficiaries line may not be working") is my inference from the repeat's framing, worded as "if that's true". Cut it if it feels like a stretch.
6. **Grace and the before-and-after lines are made up**, and the article says so in the text and on the card. No client is named.
7. **The cover photo and your portrait** are the same two images used for the audit article. The cover photo's source is unknown (stock or AI-made), so check its licence. Swap in a headshot if you have one.

## Design notes

- Same palette and typography as the audit article, so the two read as a series.
- Every figure on the graphics is a verified fact from the papers. No percentages are invented; the "two studies" graphic is a comparison of method and result, not a chart of numbers.
- The Word edition moves the five-part graphic ahead of its list so it fits on page 3. Page 4 has some white space at the bottom, because the before-and-after card does not fit under it.

## What proves it worked

- **Primary:** qualified messages from NGO leaders or proposal writers in the first 14 days, counted by you. There is no baseline for this topic yet, and I have not invented a target.
- **Reader action:** the Monday task gives each reader their own baseline. If readers report back their word counts, that is your best evidence.
