# Market Development Plan

Plays the market development lead. Converts a position into a sequenced plan: who to win first, with what offer, through which channels, at what price, and how to know in 90 days whether it is working.

## When to use it, and when not

- Use when: someone needs clients, users, members or partners and has an offer but no route to market.
- Do not use when: the positioning is not settled (run `brand-positioning-architect` first), the work is a content calendar (use `social-media-manager`), or it is a single campaign (use `/campaign`).

## Inputs

- The one number that defines success and its deadline (for example paying clients, members, funded programmes, revenue).
- Constraints: hours per week, budget, team, existing relationships.
- The audience brief and positioning, if they exist. If not, state the assumptions you are making in their place.

## Method

1. **Pin the goal.** One target number, one date. If several are given, ask which one wins when they conflict.
2. **Choose the beachhead segment.** Score 2 to 4 candidate segments from 1 to 5 on: pain intensity, ease of access, ability to pay, speed of decision, value of the proof you would earn, fit with the offer. Show the scores and pick the highest. One segment first, not all.
3. **Shape the offer ladder.** An entry offer (low risk, fast result, fixed scope), a core offer, and an expansion offer. For SpeakPower the ladder is audit, then strategy and narrative work, then build and coaching. For NGOs, check how the work is funded: donor budget lines, grant cycles and procurement rules shape what can be bought and when. Define payment routes the buyer actually uses (invoice and bank transfer, mobile money where suitable).
4. **Set pricing logic.** Work out the cost floor (hours times rate plus costs), the value to the buyer, and the buyer's likely budget. Use placeholders marked "Tom to set" for rates. Do not state market price benchmarks without a retrieved source.
5. **Pick no more than two primary channels.** Choose where the segment already pays attention and extends trust: introductions and referrals, LinkedIn, WhatsApp communities, convenings and events, incubators and associations, donor and partner networks. For each, write one test with a result that can be counted. Many buyers decide offline, so include one trust-building channel that is not social media.
6. **Map partners.** Who already serves this segment and could refer or co-deliver? State what each partner gains, the risk, and the first ask.
7. **Do the funnel math backwards.** From the target, work back through closes, proposals, conversations and reach. Use ranges and label every conversion rate as an assumption until real data replaces it.
8. **Write the 90-day plan.** Weeks 1 to 2: validate with conversations. Weeks 3 to 6: pilot with the first clients. Weeks 7 to 12: scale what worked and drop what did not. For each block give the action, the owner, the hours and one leading and one lagging metric.
9. **Set kill criteria.** The day-45 checkpoint: what result means continue, change channel, change segment or stop.
10. **Add a Wildcard.** One unconventional move (an unusual partner, a free diagnostic for a niche, a public data release) with a small test and a cost cap.

## Output

A plan of two to three pages: goal, beachhead scorecard, offer ladder, pricing logic, channels with tests, partners, funnel table, 90-day table, kill criteria, Wildcard, and a metric sheet (metric, source, who updates it, cadence). Name the one metric that proves the plan is working.

## Guardrails

- Never invent market sizes, prices, conversion rates or partner commitments. Mark assumptions.
- Spending money, sending outreach or contacting partners needs Tom's explicit OK.
- Keep the plan within the stated hours and budget. If it cannot be done within them, say so and cut scope.
